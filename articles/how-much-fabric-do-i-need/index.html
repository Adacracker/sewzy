How Much Fabric Do I Need? A Simple Yardage Guide

4 min read · Updated 2026-10-05

Buying fabric without knowing how much to get is stressful. Buy too little and you cannot finish. Buy too much and you pay for fabric you do not need. The good news is that working out fabric yardage is simple once you know the few things that matter.

Start with the pattern's fabric chart

Any good pattern includes a chart that lists the amount of fabric by size. Always begin there. The chart already considers the pattern pieces and the usual fabric width. Match your size and fabric width, and you have a reliable starting number.

Pay attention to which width the chart uses. Fabric in a shop is sold off a bolt, and the width is not always the same.

Know your fabric width

Fabric width is the distance from one finished edge (selvage) to the other. Common widths include:

  • Quilting cotton: about 42 to 44 inches (around 110 cm).
  • Dress and apparel fabric: often 54 to 60 inches (around 140 to 150 cm).
  • Home decor fabric: usually wider again.

If your fabric is narrower than the chart assumes, you will probably need more length. If it is wider, you may need less. Check the label on the bolt before you ask for a length.

Yards, metres and conversions

A yard is 36 inches, which is about 0.91 metres. A metre is about 1.09 yards. When you are converting, round up to the next quarter yard or the next ten centimetres. Shops usually cut in those steps, so this saves you from being a little short.

Plan your own layout

For small projects that do not have a chart, sketch a layout. Measure each pattern piece at its longest and widest points. Draw a rectangle that matches the fabric width and place the pieces inside it, keeping the grain line parallel to the selvage. Then measure the length you used. This quick drawing is what a fabric calculator does for you.

Remember to include a seam allowance if the pieces in your plan do not already have it.

Things that add to the amount you need

Shrinkage

Cotton can shrink when washed, so buy a little extra, about 5 to 10 percent, and pre-wash before cutting. Pre-washing means your finished project will not change size on its first wash.

Pattern matching

Stripes, plaids and large prints need to line up across seams. This takes extra fabric, often an additional quarter to half a yard for small projects. For a big garment, add more.

One-way prints

A print with a direction, such as flowers that point up, must be cut with all the pieces facing the same way. That limits how you can place pieces, so you need extra length.

Nap and pile

Velvet, corduroy and fleece have a direction. Pieces must all be cut the same way, so allow for extra fabric.

Mistakes

If you are a beginner, add a little for mistakes. A spare quarter yard is cheap insurance when you are learning.

Do not forget the extras

Lining, interfacing, batting, bias binding and elastic are all separate. Check the pattern's supplies list and note each of them. For quilted items, you will need top fabric, backing and batting, and the backing is the same size as the top, plus a little.

A worked example

Say a tote bag needs two outer pieces and two lining pieces, each 16 by 18 inches, plus two straps. Fabric at 44 inches wide fits two 16 inch pieces side by side, so you can cut both outer pieces in a single 18 inch row, and both lining pieces in another. That is 36 inches for the bag body, or one yard, plus straps and a margin for shrinkage. So buy a little over a yard of each fabric. A quick sketch makes numbers like this obvious.

Buy the right amount, not the exact amount

Add a small safety margin and keep leftovers. Offcuts make pouches, appliqué, pockets and patchwork, so extra fabric is rarely wasted. If the fabric is a one-off and the shop is far away, buy a bit more than you think you need.

FAQ

How do I work out how much fabric I need?

Start with the fabric chart in the pattern for your size and fabric width. For small projects without a chart, sketch the pieces inside a rectangle the width of the fabric and measure the length.

Should I buy extra fabric?

Yes. Add about 5 to 10 percent for shrinkage, and more for stripes, plaids, one-way prints or fabrics with a nap.

What is the difference between yards and metres?

A yard is 36 inches or about 0.91 metres. A metre is about 1.09 yards. Round up when converting.

Does fabric width change the amount I need?

Yes. Narrower fabric usually needs more length, and wider fabric may need less. Always check the width on the bolt.
