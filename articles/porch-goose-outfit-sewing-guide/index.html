Porch Goose Outfits: A Sewing Guide for Goose Clothes

5 min read · Updated 2026-10-05

Dressing a concrete porch goose is one of the happiest little sewing projects around. The goose stays still, never wriggles, and the neighbours love seeing what it is wearing this week. If you have been searching for a goose clothes pattern, this guide walks you through how an outfit is built and how to get a good result on your first try.

How a porch goose outfit is put together

Most goose outfits share the same simple structure. A fitted top section wraps the body, a gathered skirt hangs from it, and small extras such as a hat, a bow or a collar finish the look. Because the goose is a solid statue, there is no stretch to rely on. The fit comes entirely from measuring well and cutting accurately.

The outfit has a short slit down the back, closed with a ribbon bow. That slit is what lets the garment slip over the goose's head and settle on the body. Once it is in place, you tie the bow and the back looks neat.

Pick the right size first

Porch geese are usually sold in three heights: 17, 23 and 27 inch. The patterns come in three matching sizes, so the first job is to know which goose you own. Measure the goose from the base to the top of its head, then measure around the widest part of the body and around the head. Our guide on how to measure a porch goose shows each step.

Write the numbers down before you buy any fabric. A goose that is between sizes is better dressed in the larger size with a slightly deeper bow tie than squeezed into the smaller one.

Choose fabric that behaves outdoors

Porch geese live on porches, steps and gardens, so think about weather. Tightly woven cotton, cotton blends and quilting cotton are friendly to sew and hold their shape. Felt works well for small details like hat brims and appliqué because the edges do not fray.

  • Body pieces: a medium-weight woven cotton gives clean seams and good structure.
  • Skirts: lighter cottons gather softly and look full without bulk.
  • Details: felt, ribbon and bias tape for trims.

Pre-wash cotton before cutting so it does not shrink on the first rainy day. If the outfit will live outdoors, plan to bring it inside in heavy rain and wash it gently when it gets dusty.

Sewing gathered skirt tiers

Gathered tiers are what give goose skirts their charm, and they are easier than they look. Here is the basic method:

  1. Set your machine to the longest straight stitch.
  2. Sew two parallel rows along the top edge of the tier, about 1/4 inch and 1/2 inch from the edge. Leave long thread tails.
  3. Pull the two bobbin threads together gently, sliding the fabric along until the tier matches the length it needs to join to.
  4. Spread the gathers evenly, pin often, then sew with a normal stitch length between your two rows.
  5. Press the seam toward the upper piece and remove any gathering thread that shows.

When a skirt has several tiers, finish each tier join before adding the next. Press as you go, because a pressed seam makes the next step much easier.

The back slit and bow

Take your time with the slit. Reinforce the bottom of it with a few backstitches so it does not tear when you pull the outfit over the head. Hem the edges with a narrow double fold, then sew ribbon ties at the top so the bow sits neatly. Always test the outfit on the goose before you add the final trim, so you can adjust the opening if needed.

Pick your first outfit

If you are new to goose clothes, start with something small and quick so you learn the shape of the pieces. The Goose Easy Dress is a beginner-friendly choice. The Porch Goose Outfit is a classic base look once you want a little more structure, and the Porch Goose Accessories Set adds hats and extras to dress it up.

For the seasons, there are plenty of themed looks, including the Pumpkin Outfit, the Witch Outfit, the Ghost Outfit and the Santa Outfit.

Finishing touches that make it look polished

Trim threads closely, press every seam, and check the hem is even all the way around. Stand back and look at the goose from the street, since that is how most people will see it. Bold trims and clear shapes read better from a distance than tiny details.

FAQ

What sizes do porch goose outfits come in?

The goose patterns come in three sizes, made for 17, 23 and 27 inch lawn geese. Measure your goose from base to top of head to pick the right one.

Do I need to be an expert to sew goose clothes?

No. Several outfits are marked beginner level, and the basic skills are straight seams, gathering and a simple hem. Start with an easy dress, then move on to themed outfits.

How does the outfit go on the goose?

Each outfit has a short back slit. You slip it over the head, settle it on the body and close the slit with a ribbon bow.

What fabric is best for goose clothes?

Medium-weight cotton or cotton blends for the body, lighter cotton for gathered skirts and felt for small details. Pre-wash cotton before cutting.
